How these numbers are made

  • Content is graded by a language model running on this computer, against a rubric: technical accuracy 40%, problem solving 30%, completeness 20%, optimization 10%. The weighted score is calculated, not written by the model. These scores have not been checked against human interviewers.
  • Follow-up questions are written by the same local model from what you said. Your main answer is graded the moment it ends; the follow-up answer gets its own short score.
  • Interruptions happen when an answer passes two minutes of speaking (one for a follow-up) or starts repeating itself: a third or more of your recent three-word phrases were already said earlier. Both come from your words and timing, never your face.
  • Pace, pauses and fillers come from word timings in a transcript made on this computer (Whisper). Speech recognition tidies up some "um"s and "uh"s, so the filler count is a lower bound.
  • On camera is the share of time your head and eyes pointed where they did during calibration. Glasses, low light and a camera far to one side make it less accurate.
  • The guide ranges (120–160 words a minute, under 3 fillers a minute, on camera at least 60% of the time) are rules of thumb, not grades. DeepHire never infers emotions or "confidence" from your face.
